# Editorial pass: useful, specific and truthful

This is a quality standard, not an AI-authorship test. Do not use detector
scores, fake anecdotes, deliberate errors or arbitrary punctuation changes to
make copy "pass as human". Fluent human copy can contain cliches; AI assistance
does not by itself make useful, accurate content bad.

## What each paragraph must do

It should answer a real question, explain a useful distinction, supply evidence
or help a decision. Cut paragraphs that only announce the topic, praise the
offer or repeat an earlier claim. Lead with the answer/offer and concrete
details; add background when the audience actually needs it.

Prefer verbs, named subjects, examples and explicit limits over adjective
stacks and abstractions. Preserve necessary domain terminology and authentic
brand language. Write naturally in the audience's language; do not blindly
apply English word filters to multilingual content.

## Patterns to challenge in context

- Empty openings: "In today's rapidly evolving landscape", "In the digital age".
- Hype without proof: "game-changing", "revolutionary", "world-class",
  "unparalleled", "cutting-edge", "best institute", "industry-leading".
- Vague promises: "unlock your potential", "transform your journey",
  "seamlessly leverage the power", "future-proof your career".
- Repetitive cadence: every section has the same introduction, three shallow
  bullets and a summary; every sentence begins with "Whether you're...".
- Manufactured contrast: "This isn't just X; it's Y" without a useful distinction.
- Stacked synonyms, inflated transitions, redundant conclusions and filler FAQs
  that repeat a keyword without answering a customer's question.
- Copy that could swap the business name for any competitor's with no change.

These are review prompts, not a blacklist. Keep a word when it conveys specific
meaning. Removing "leverage" alone does not fix an empty paragraph.

## Fact discipline

Maintain a compact evidence ledger: claim, supplied/cited source, status and
editorial treatment. Treat proposed curricula, unconfirmed fees and tentative
policies as such. Do not invent expertise, years, learner counts, rankings,
testimonials, placements, salaries, awards, accreditations, client results or
firsthand tests. Source-backed business statements remain self-reported unless
independently checked; significant conflicting sources belong in the audit.

For an unsupported claim, remove the claim or use a truthful neutral sentence.
If it is essential to the decision, ask for the fact. Keep the evidence request
outside the publishable draft. Do not invent a plausible concrete fact merely
to replace vague copy. Never change commercial terms or guarantees silently.

## Example: same supplied facts, better copy

Supplied facts for this example only: a Python course covers functions and file
handling; its project is a CSV cleaner; learners receive project feedback.

Before: "Unlock your potential with our cutting-edge, industry-leading Python
course and embark on a transformative journey toward career success."

After: "Learn Python functions and file handling by building a CSV cleaner.
You'll submit the project and receive feedback on your code."

The improvement comes from supported particulars. If the project and feedback
were not supplied, do not make them up; request course details instead.

## Editing and final pass

1. Preserve source facts, scope, caveats and useful examples.
2. Put the reader's answer and decision criteria in a sensible order.
3. Replace generic language with supported specifics; cut empty claims.
4. Remove duplicated concepts and forced keywords without deleting necessary
   detail. There is no default target density, word count or paragraph pattern.
5. Check title and description against the actual offer. They must not promise
   content, prices or results absent from the page.
6. Read the finished copy as a person making the relevant decision. Check that
   it sounds like this business, answers the question, and can be acted on.

Google's quality guidance favors original, useful information and clear
evidence of experience or expertise; it does not prescribe a preferred word
count. These editorial rules apply that guidance without turning it into a
numerical ranking formula. [Helpful content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

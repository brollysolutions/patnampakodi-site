# Competitor analysis with a defensible outcome

Analyze competing pages for the chosen reader task, not just competitor domains.
Business rivals, search competitors and AI-cited sources may be different sets.

## Select the comparison set

Use current results for representative intent clusters. Usually inspect 3-5
relevant pages per distinct page type, reusing a page across queries when useful;
expand when markets or intents differ. This is a sampling starting point, not
a mandatory quota or an exhaustive-market claim. Include directories, publishers,
marketplaces or documentation when they compete for the task. Keep ads and local
pack observations separate from organic page results.

Record query, exact URL, competitor category, date, tool, market/device certainty,
result feature and access status. A generic search sample cannot establish a
precise localized Google rank. Compare like markets and offers where possible;
explain when a major publisher is an intent reference rather than a realistic
commercial competitor.

## Inspect each page on common dimensions

| Dimension | What to capture | Decision it supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intent and audience | Task answered, page type, assumed knowledge, buyer stage | Whether our proposed page belongs in the result set |
| Offer and answer | Main answer, scope, inclusions, verified pricing basis, limitations | The facts a reader expects to find |
| Coverage | Useful questions, comparisons, steps, examples and missing explanations | Material answer gaps, without copying an outline |
| Proof and originality | Named sources, demonstrated work, methodology, actual examples, dates | What evidence we need to earn trust |
| Information architecture | Heading meaning, pathways to related pages, descriptive anchors | Better discovery and progression through the task |
| UX and decisions | Mobile readability, tables, disclosures, CTA destination and friction | A concrete improvement in the reader's journey |
| Technical source | Available metadata, canonical, structured data and render evidence | Confirmed page behavior versus unsupported technical guesses |
| Performance | Comparable measured lab/field evidence, if available | A measured constraint, not an inference from visual complexity |
| Authority and links | Observed citations/referring pages or authorized backlink export | Relevant sources/assets worth earning; no invented domain scores |
| Freshness | Material updates and whether facts need recency | Actual stale facts; changing dates alone is not added value |

Use one row per competitor page and evidence IDs/links. Distinguish a company's
self-reported claims from verified observations. “Not found on the inspected
page” does not mean “the business does not offer this.” Inaccessible pages remain
unreviewed, even when their snippets look informative.

## Turn comparison into opportunities

For each opportunity state:

1. The reader's unmet question or decision difficulty.
2. The pages/sections establishing that gap and sampling limitations.
3. What the user's business can truthfully contribute: documented process,
   original demonstration, expert explanation, verified terms or useful tool.
4. The evidence still needed and who could supply it; do not invent an owner.
5. The target URL and proposed section, interaction or supporting asset.
6. Effort, confidence and how improvement will be checked.

Distinguish **parity essentials** (accurate useful details readers reasonably
need), **differentiation** (supported original value), **irrelevant features**
and **unverified hypotheses**. The recommendation should survive removing the
competitor's brand name from the table.

## Backlinks, reputation and defensibility

When authorized data exists, compare referring-page relevance, editorial context,
destination usefulness, lost/broken references and genuinely linkable original
assets. Provider indexes are partial and authority metrics are proprietary
estimates. Do not diagnose a penalty from a low score or recommend disavowal from
a generic “toxic links” number. Escalate an evidenced manual-action issue for a
separate review. No purchased ranking links, fabricated reviews, automated
outreach, copied assets or reputation attacks.

The output is an actionable comparison and opportunity backlog, not a claim that
matching competitor word counts, headings or schema will reproduce their rank.
Respect [Google's spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).
